# 02 · Translating your school's policy

*The Massive AI Manual — Student Papers guide*

School AI policies are written by committees, in lawyer-adjacent English, and
rarely name a specific tool. This guide is a decoder. Last step: ask the person
who grades your work — **your teacher's word overrides this manual.**

---

## Decoder ring: policy phrases → plain English

| Policy phrase | What it usually means | Safe move |
|---------------|----------------------|-----------|
| "The work must be the student's own." | You wrote the sentences and understand the ideas. AI may tutor; it may not tap the keys. | Write it yourself. |
| "AI assistance is not permitted." | Don't paste the assignment into an AI tool at all — not even for brainstorming. | Pen, paper, library, office hours. |
| "AI may be used per instructor direction." | The default is **no** until your teacher says otherwise, per assignment. | Ask: "Did you mean this assignment too?" |
| "Grammarly/spellcheck-level assistance only." | Fixing errors in your own sentences: yes. Generating content: no. | Clarify whether paraphrasing tools count. |
| "Ideation and outlining are permitted with citation." | You can brainstorm and outline with AI, but must do the writing *and* disclose it. | Follow [guide 06](06-disclosure-statements.md). |
| "AI use must be disclosed." | Tell them, in writing, in the assignment — not later, not vaguely. | One clear sentence. |
| "Undisclosed AI use will be treated as academic misconduct." | Discovery without disclosure = cheating case, even if the use itself was green-lit. | Disclose generously. Over-disclosing is never penalized; under-disclosing is. |
| "The student must be able to explain their submitted work." | Be ready to answer "why did you argue X?" without notes. | Know your paper. That's the real test. |

## The five questions to ask your teacher

Bring these the first week. They make you look serious, not suspicious:

1. "For this assignment, is AI allowed for **brainstorming and outlining**, or
   writing only, or not at all?"
2. "Is a **grammar checker** okay? What about paraphrasing tools?"
3. "If yes: what **disclosure statement** do you want to see, and where?"
4. "Can I paste my **draft** into AI for feedback, or must feedback be human?"
5. "Will parts of this be **done in class or discussed** with me?" (This is how
   your school actually checks understanding — know it, and lose nothing.)

Write the answers on the first page of your notebook. That page is your
personal policy, and it beats any guesswork for the rest of the term.

## Thirty-second risk test

Before you submit anything AI touched:

- [ ] Could I explain every paragraph of this paper if called on in class?
- [ ] Is every citation real, and did I actually read the sources?
- [ ] Did I follow my teacher's answers to the five questions?
- [ ] If AI contributed, is my disclosure statement in place?
- [ ] Would I be comfortable if the AI chat history were part of my grade?

**All five checked: you're fine. Any unchecked box: fix it before submitting.**

## Common honest misunderstandings

These get real students in trouble even with good intentions:

1. **"The tool said my school's name, so it knew the policy."** AI chatbots
   invent plausible-sounding policies. Never take policy advice *from* the AI —
   take it from the syllabus ([guide 05](05-verifying-citations.md) explains why
   AI invents things).
2. **"My friend said our teacher doesn't check."** Honor codes don't include a
   "not checked" clause — and they include you, not just your teacher.
3. **"I only used it to fix my English."** If a sentence you'd never say
   out loud is now nodding along with the tool, that's not your English
   anymore. Editing your own voice is fine; adding a voice that isn't
   yours isn't.
4. **"I disclosed it verbally in office hours."** Good start — also put it in
   the document. Written disclosure survives human memory.
5. **"Group project, so someone's AI use is shared."** Everyone attached to
   undisclosed AI use usually shares the consequences. Share this manual with
   your team first.
