The Massive AI Manual — Student Papers guide

This is the rule everything else in this manual hangs on. Master it and you never need to memorize a table again.

The rule

A tutor gets paid when you don't need it anymore. A ghostwriter gets paid when you can't.

Use AI like the first one. Never like the second.

A good tutor: explains the material, asks you the questions, stumps you on purpose, and celebrates when you write the answer yourself. It never writes your paragraph.

A ghostwriter: takes the assignment, hands back finished prose, and leaves you unable to explain what's in it.

Both fulfill the request. Only one leaves you passing the follow-up questions.

The line, drawn precisely

The tutor asks...The ghostwriter asks...
"What do you think the author means here?""What should the paragraph say?"
"Teach me the concept so I can write it.""Write the concept in academic tone."
"Here's my outline — poke holes in it.""Build me an outline I can copy."
"Grade my argument like a strict grader.""Make it sound smarter."
"Quiz me until I can do this unaided.""Just handle the hard parts."

Test any request before you send it: If the AI answered perfectly, would I be smarter or would my homework be done for me? Smart → tutor. Done-for-me → ghostwriter.

Tutor prompts you can steal

Paste these into any AI chat and fill in the blanks. None of them write a word of your paper.

Explain-before-you-write

"Explain [TOPIC] to me like I'm smart but new to it. Use one concrete example. Then ask me two questions to check I actually understood."

Socratic setup

"I have to write about [TOPIC]. Don't write anything for me. Ask me questions, one at a time, until we uncover an argument I can defend. If I give a weak answer, push back once, then ask a better question."

Gap-finder (pre-writing)

"Here's my outline: [PASTE OUTLINE]. Don't rewrite it. List the three biggest holes a skeptical professor would attack, then ask me how I'd fix each one."

Draft-doctor (post-writing)

"Here's my draft: [PASTE DRAFT]. Act like a demanding professor. (1) Point out the two weakest claims and why. (2) Show me where I assert instead of argue. (3) Ask me two questions I'd be scared to answer in office hours. Do not rewrite sentences for me."

Office-hours rehearsal

"Quiz me on my own paper. Random questions, like a viva. If I can't answer one, mark it — I'll go back and understand it before I submit anything."

Citation-smell-test

"Here's my bibliography: [PASTE]. List which claims in my paper each source must actually support. I'll pull the real sources and check every one myself." (Then do — see guide 05.)

The tell-tale signs you've drifted to ghostwriter-land

Catch yourself early:

  1. You can't summarize your own thesis out loud. The thesis appeared in the chat window before it appeared in you.
  2. Vocabulary you'd never use out loud. "Multifaceted," "myriad," and "delve" appearing together is a machine fingerprint.
  3. You're editing AI text, not writing. Rearranging sentences the bot produced is not writing — it's laundering.
  4. The chat history is more work than the paper. Nine back-and-forths to "improve paragraph 3 as much as possible" is ghostwriting with extra steps.
  5. You'd delete the chat history if asked for it. If you'd rather the teacher not see the conversation, the conversation is the problem.
  6. The paper knows things you don't. Classes always check: in-class writing, discussions, follow-up questions (detailed in guide 01).

When it is completely fine to ask AI to write things

These are things that aren't graded as your thinking:

  • Practice materials. Quizzes, flashcards, model answers you then critique.
  • Anything you then tear apart. "Write a bad example of this essay, so I can label what's wrong with it."
  • Non-submitted text: emails to your professor, study-group plans, schedules.
  • With explicit permission and disclosure, in assignments where your teacher allows some AI drafting — but treat even authorized AI drafting as gray zone, because an essay you can't defend fails you anyway when the teacher asks.
